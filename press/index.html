In two sentences

Most travel apps send your location to a server. TripRewind doesn't — there are no servers. It records everywhere you go, links it up with notes, keeps it privately on your phone, and lets you relive any journey with your photos placed exactly where you took them.

What it does

Records

Runs in the background, tracking GPS position, speed, stops, and activity. Works without mobile data. Survives a phone restart. Uses roughly the same battery as Google Maps (~5% per hour).

Stores

Every GPS point stored locally in an encrypted database on your phone. No account. No cloud sync. No one else's servers.

Relives

Open the app and your complete journey is on a map. Every road, every stop, every place you stayed. Notes appear as pins. Photos from your camera roll drop onto the map at the exact spot you took them, matched by GPS and timestamp entirely on your device.

Exports

GPX (hiking devices, mapping apps), CSV (spreadsheets), JSON (developers), KML (Google Earth flythrough animations).

Backs up

Your entire trip history in a single AES-256-GCM encrypted file you send wherever you like. Recovery phrase in your password manager means you can restore to a new phone at any time.

Standout features

Everything stays on your phone

Your route, your notes, your photos — stored locally in an encrypted database. No TripRewind account, no server, no cloud. Your travel history is yours the moment you record it, and it stays that way.

Photos, placed where you took them

Open a trip map and your photos from that journey appear pinned to the exact spot you took them. Matched by GPS and timestamp, entirely on your device. No upload, no cloud processing.

Trips that match how you actually travel

A trip can cover six weeks or six minutes, and they can overlap freely. "Europe 2025" and "Day in Porto" share the same GPS points — TripRewind shows both, no hierarchy required.

Notes by voice or keyboard

Drop a note at any moment — whether recording or not. Speak it or type it; it pins to the map exactly where you were.

Stops, automatic

Stayed somewhere for 20 minutes? TripRewind detects it and labels it on the map with how long you were there. Every coffee stop, every viewpoint, every airport wait — accounted for, without you doing anything.

Share the journey, not the data

The Rewind share card puts your route, stats, and photos into a single image you send wherever you like. Built entirely on your device — no server involved.

The privacy angle

Most GPS apps send location data to a server. TripRewind has no servers. There is no TripRewind account, no database with your movements, nothing to breach. The GPS track of your holiday, the photo at the summit, the note you dropped at that restaurant — none of it leaves your device unless you export it yourself.

This isn't a privacy setting. It's the architecture.

Who uses it

  • Travellers who want a permanent record of every trip
  • Hikers and walkers logging trails, distances, and elevation
  • Road trippers mapping the route they drove
  • Grey Nomads keeping the Big Lap forever
  • Anyone who lost Google Timeline data and wants something they own

Origin story

The developer lost months of Google Timeline data when Google changed how it worked. Rather than hoping a tech company would keep the record, they built something that would keep it on the phone — forever. TripRewind is that app.
